# Research-gap tests

## Gap taxonomy

- `empirical`: a claim lacks adequate tests, replications, uncertainty, or negative results.
- `methodological`: relevant alternatives, ablations, or causal attribution are missing.
- `context`: evidence is bounded to a population, domain, language, embodiment, sensor, or operating condition.
- `evaluation`: metrics or protocols do not measure the capability being claimed.
- `integration`: components exist separately but their interaction or system-level effect is untested.
- `translation`: offline/simulation evidence does not establish real-world, closed-loop, safety, latency, or maintainability behavior.
- `theoretical`: assumptions, identifiability, convergence, or mechanism remain unresolved.

## Falsification questions

For each candidate ask:

1. What exact claim says the gap exists?
2. Which included papers support it, with locators?
3. What terminology might make contrary work easy to miss?
4. Which targeted queries and sources could disconfirm it?
5. Does a recent paper fully close, partially close, or merely mention it?
6. Is the remaining scope important and testable with available resources?
7. What result would show that the proposed contribution adds no value?

## Status rules

- `supported`: multiple primary sources expose the boundary and targeted search found no work that closes the narrowly stated gap.
- `provisional`: evidence is plausible but source/access/query coverage is incomplete.
- `rejected`: contrary work closes the proposed gap or the statement depended on an invalid comparison.
- `insufficient-search`: the search space, terminology, or full-text access is too weak for a novelty claim.

Never translate `supported` into “the first ever.” It means defensible within the recorded scope and date.
