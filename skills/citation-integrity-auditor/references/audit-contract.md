# Citation audit contract

## Source identity check

Prefer, in order: publisher/proceedings record, DOI registry metadata, official repository or preprint record, institutional repository, then scholarly indexes. Record conflicts rather than choosing the most convenient metadata. A project page or code repository can support software facts but normally cannot substitute for the paper's experimental evidence.

## Status check

Inspect authoritative records for correction, erratum, expression of concern, retraction, withdrawal, and version history. A retracted work may be discussed as retracted evidence but must not silently support a positive scientific claim.

## Claim-entailment check

For each claim ask:

1. Is this the exact source intended by the citation?
2. What passage, table, figure, equation, or appendix is the evidence?
3. Does it support the complete claim or only one clause?
4. Are population, dataset, baseline, metric, statistical uncertainty, and experimental conditions preserved?
5. Is the claim the authors' result, their interpretation, or the current writer's inference?
6. Is there nearby qualifying or contrary evidence?

## Claims requiring strict locators

- numerical results and comparisons;
- causal or mechanism claims;
- novelty, priority, and field-wide absence claims;
- author-stated limitations;
- safety, generalization, real-world, and deployment claims;
- quotations and close paraphrases.

## Audit record

For every changed citation or claim, preserve the original, the problem, the verified source/locator, the minimal revision, and the resulting verdict. Do not erase failed checks; they explain why the final text changed.
