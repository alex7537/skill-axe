# Academic argument contract

## Claim roles

- `context`: bounded fact establishing the problem setting.
- `achievement`: what an approach demonstrated under named conditions.
- `limitation`: an author-stated or synthesis-supported boundary.
- `gap`: a literature-coverage claim validated by targeted search.
- `inference`: the writer's synthesis; identify it as interpretation when the sources do not state it directly.
- `proposal`: the thesis question, hypothesis, method, or evaluation plan; never present as completed evidence.

## Paragraph test

Every paragraph should have one argumentative job, a clear topic sentence, evidence tied to its premises, and a closing sentence that advances the reasoning. Prefer a chain such as:

`problem → current approaches → demonstrated value → bounded limitation → unresolved need → thesis question → test`

The limitation must follow from evidence, and the proposed work must actually test the stated gap.

## Citation placement

Cite immediately after the smallest supported claim. Split sentences when different clauses require different sources. Preserve claim IDs during drafting even if the final citation style later changes. A bibliography entry without an in-text supported claim does not repair the argument.

## Bilingual parity

Check proper nouns, method names, numerical conditions, modal strength, causal language, novelty, and citation placement. Record an intentional adaptation when Chinese and English require different syntax, but never different evidence scope.
